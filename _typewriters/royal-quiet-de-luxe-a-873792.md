A 1939 Royal Quiet De Luxe carrying **Vogue** — Royal's own name for the
typeface, and the reason this machine gets a page. Vogue is uncommon on any
machine. On a Quiet De Luxe it is more uncommon still; where you normally find it
is on a Royal Model P.

It types perfectly. There is nothing wrong with it.

## Vogue

Vogue is a geometric Art Deco face. My reading of its provenance is that it comes
from **Kabel** — Rudolf Koch's 1927 geometric sans — modified by Royal for use as
a typewriter face, though I haven't chased that to a primary source.

## Identifying it

Vogue is identified **from the printed type, or from the type slugs themselves**.
The slugs also carry a foundry mark, but you don't need it — the face is
distinctive enough to call from a sheet of typing.

That matters if you're trying to work out what you have. A machine is not
labelled "Vogue" anywhere on the outside; it is a typeface option, not a model.

## Round shift keys, 1939 only

The body is an otherwise standard Quiet De Luxe, with one exception worth
knowing: **for 1939 alone, Royal used round shift keys instead of rectangular
ones.** One year, then back.

It's a small thing that makes a 1939 machine identifiable across a room, and it's
half the reason I like this one — the typeface being the other half.

## Finding it

It came off a local Craigslist listing, minutes from where I was living in
Danville at the time. The seller had posted three machines and this one was
barely pictured — a friend spotted the listing and sent it to me, and I would
very likely have missed it otherwise.

A widow was selling her late husband's machines, found in his office. My
impression is that they had been on display rather than in use.

She asked less than I paid. I knew what the machine was and she didn't, and I
had only hundreds on me, so I told her to keep the difference. Even then it was
far below what a Vogue-equipped machine is worth — which is the part that sits
oddly. Knowing more than the person selling to you is an advantage you can
choose not to take all of.

## Condition

**Cleaned, serviced and detailed by me, and fully functional.** It came with its
case.

Platen and feed rollers were **recovered professionally by JJ Short**. The feet I
reprinted in TPU myself — an ordinary consumable rubber part, easier to print
than to source, and nothing to do with type.

## A note on the Vogue Slug

The [type element project]({{ '/projects/type-elements/' | relative_url }})
includes a **Vogue Slug**, which is *not* a part for this machine and not a
replacement for anything on it. It's a standalone reference piece carrying the
digitised Vogue face, made because Vogue is sought after enough to be worth
having as an object in its own right.

Worth stating plainly, because the shared name invites exactly the wrong
conclusion.
