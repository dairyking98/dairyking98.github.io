The machine that started the [type element project]({{ '/projects/type-elements/' | relative_url }}).
A Blickensderfer No. 5 from 1909, on Blickensderfer's own DHIATENSOR keyboard
rather than QWERTY.

{% include machine-spec.html %}

## Why this one

Second in my own ranking of the collection, for the printed elements, the
uniqueness, and the age.

The Blickensderfer is a genuinely different machine rather than a variation on
the standard theme. Instead of a basket of typebars it uses a single cylindrical
**typewheel** carrying every character, which rotates and tilts to present the
right one — the same principle IBM would later reach for with the Selectric's
typeball, arrived at some sixty years earlier. It inks from a roller rather than
a ribbon, and the whole machine has far fewer parts than a contemporary
typebar portable.

The keyboard is the other departure. DHIATENSOR puts the most frequently used
letters on the bottom row, on the argument that this is where the fingers rest
most comfortably — a rival efficiency claim to QWERTY, made decades before Dvorak.

## The typewheel

A worn or cracked typewheel is the usual reason a Blickensderfer sits unused:
they are consumable parts, machine-specific, and no longer made. That is exactly
what the type element project set out to fix — a parametric OpenSCAD typewheel,
printed in resin, dimensioned from this machine.
