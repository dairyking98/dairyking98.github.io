    Why this one

    Second in my own ranking of the collection, for the printed elements, the uniqueness, and the age.

    The Blickensderfer is a genuinely different machine rather than a variation on the standard theme. Instead of a basket of typebars it uses a single cylindrical typewheel carrying every character, which rotates and tilts to present the right one — the same principle IBM would later reach for with the Selectric’s typeball, arrived at some sixty years earlier. It inks from a roller rather than a ribbon, and the whole machine has far fewer parts than a contemporary typebar portable.

    The keyboard is the other departure. DHIATENSOR puts the most frequently used letters on the bottom row, on the argument that this is where the fingers rest most comfortably — a rival efficiency claim to QWERTY, made decades before Dvorak.

    The typewheel

    A worn or cracked typewheel is the usual reason a Blickensderfer sits unused: they are consumable parts, machine-specific, and no longer made. That is exactly what the type element project set out to fix — a parametric OpenSCAD typewheel, printed in resin, dimensioned from this machine.

    Restoration

    New rubber: platen flattened, feed rollers, and feet. Outstanding: the ink roller height needs setting — it currently inks unevenly across the wheel.
