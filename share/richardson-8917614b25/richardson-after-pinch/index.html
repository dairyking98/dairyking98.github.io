Richardson selection: motion after the pinch¶

How to get the lock pin, hammer trip and other actions out of the key stroke once the plates have stopped. Everything here is a first estimate, with no CAD or test behind it.

A correction first. I suggested cutting an arc, centred on the pivot rod, into the top of each lever's riser. That doesn't work where the riser is, and the first section shows why. A real cam dwell is possible, but it needs a second part per key. There's also a simpler answer that needs no dwell at all.

Why an arc on the riser can't hold the plates¶

An arc centred on the pivot can only resist a push that points straight at the pivot, because that's the direction the arc's surface faces. On the left is our riser: the plate moves across the top of the lever, at right angles to that direction, so it would slide along the arc with nothing stopping it. On the right is the arrangement an arc needs, with the follower moving along a line through the pivot. Drag through the last 2 mm and watch it hold.

The version that does work: a two-piece key¶

To dwell, the key lever needs a cam that pushes along a line through its own pivot. So the key becomes two parts: a key lever with the cam, and a selector carrying the riser and a roller. The cam's rising part turns the selector until the plates pinch at 18 mm. Its concentric arc then holds the selector still while the key lever carries on to 20 mm, and the universal bail goes on moving with it.

The simpler answer: fire the pin inside its capture range¶

You may not need a dwell at all. The tapered lock pin can correct ±0.4 column, which is the last 0.23 mm of key depth. So if the universal bail releases a spring-loaded pin at about 17.9 mm, the wheel is already inside the capture range for every move, long or short, because the plates are tied to the key tip. The pin then finishes the job and, once seated, trips the hammer itself. The hammer can't fire until the pin is home, at any typing speed.

All the energy is stored by the bail during the stroke, which is how Richardson does it. Drag the key speed: the order never changes. Pin and hammer flight times are my guesses.

Comparing them¶

Arc on the riser Two-piece key with a cam Fire the pin inside capture
Works? No Yes Yes
Key dip 20 mm 18 mm
Added parts 28 selectors, 28 rollers, more bearings and return springs One pin spring and a trip
What it costs Moving mass, and a roller load of about 3× the tip force Trip point must repeat within about ±0.1 mm of key depth, so one adjuster
Motion available after the plates stop 2 mm of key depth None needed, because the actions are spring-fired
Failure if it's wrong Cam wear Pin misses if the trip is set too early

My recommendation is to fire the pin inside its capture range first, because it adds almost nothing and needs no change to the levers. The two-piece cam is the fallback if the trip proves too fussy, or if you later want real motion after the pinch.
